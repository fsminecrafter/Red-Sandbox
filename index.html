How to build

Parts stay loose until you glue them. Mechanisms, bearings, sensors and other devices mount straight onto whatever you click, part or floor.

The workflow

  1. Pause with Space so nothing moves while you build.
  2. Place parts by clicking the floor or another part. Turn on Auto glue (T) and each new part sticks to the part you placed it on.
  3. Glue by dropping a glue ball where parts touch. Every part it touches becomes one rigid piece. Glue that also touches the floor anchors it.
  4. Drivetrains: put a bearing or motor down, then point the Axle tool at it and the axle snaps in. Gears, cams and wheels snap onto axles and glue themselves on. Gears of the same tooth size mesh when their edges meet. A Clutch joins the shaft it sits on to what is glued to its hub, gripping as hard as its Engage signal says (made one-way in its settings, it drives the hub but lets it run ahead freely, like a starter's freewheel); a Gearbox on a frame turns an axle through it at the ratio of its gear (wire buttons to Shift up and Shift down); a UV joint carries a shaft's turning round a corner. A Pulley snaps onto an axle like a gear; with the Belt tool, click two or more pulleys in order and then the first again to loop a belt round them. Gears mesh with real teeth that push on each other; Performance mode in Settings locks their speeds together by rule instead, which is lighter and steadier. The Quad and Inline twin examples put them all together.
  5. Signals: buttons, levers and sensors have outputs; motors, injectors, igniters, solenoids and lamps have inputs. With the Wire tool (C), click an output dot, then an input dot of the same colour. Green dots carry On/Off, blue dots carry an Analog number.
  6. Gas: an injector sprays only fuel into the space on the other side of the surface it is mounted on. Fuel needs air to burn, so an engine has to breathe: a Turbo pushes air in, an Intake lets the engine suck it in by itself. An igniter sparks there. Fuel mixed with air explodes, pushes on the walls, and leaves exhaust.
  7. Liquids: water, motor oil and gasoline pour, pool and float things, and cannot be squeezed. An Outlet with nothing piped in is a tap. An Inlet takes gas or liquid from behind the surface it sits on (inside a tank, say) into its pipe; a Fluid pump or Oil pump moves liquid from what is piped to its In to what is piped to its Out: an outlet, a valve or a Sprayer. Water boils into steam on hot gas, gasoline evaporates into fuel that burns, oil burns when hot and makes parts slippery. To lead exhaust somewhere, pipe the exhaust valve to an outlet. A tap outlet standing in a box that can hold liquid (a roof is not needed) can instead fill it: set how many litres in its settings and they appear in the box when you press Play, and go again when you rewind. Burning oil makes white smoke. The Mech. pump, Mech. oil pump and Mech. turbo have no motor of their own: turn their hub with an axle, a gear or a belt and they work as fast as it really turns, and drag on it while they do.
  8. Tubes: a Cylinder can be solid, hollow (closed at both ends), open at one end like a cup, or a tube open at both ends; set it and its wall thickness in the part's settings. Bend 90° and Bend 45° are tube elbows. All of them hold gas and liquid, so you can build pipes, tanks and engine blocks out of them (walls of about 15 cm keep liquid in). A Tube pump pushes liquid along the tube it sits in, from its back end to the end its arrow points to. The Exhaust turbo is a real turbocharger: pipe exhaust valves into it and its boost out to the inlet valves, and it spins up from the exhaust.
  9. Play and watch. Slow time down, speed it up, or rewind to the moment you pressed Play.

Moving things

The Grabber pulls parts around while time runs, presses buttons and drags levers. While paused it moves whole assemblies. Move, Rotate and Size put a gizmo on the selected part. Drag an arrow, a ring or a box handle.

Career mode

Press Career in the top bar to run an electricity business: an engine turns a generator, a Transformer steps its power up to grid voltage and a Pylon sells it. Only parts you own show in the build menu; buy more (and engines, upgrades and fuel) in the full-screen Store, where orders arrive after an in-game delivery time. One real minute at 1× is one in-game hour. Power wires wear out and snap unless you replace them under Upkeep, liquid from taps is billed every hour, the Bank pays interest (even while you are away), and Challenges give you a budget and a goal. Your career is saved in this browser.

Panels

Drag the edge or corner of a panel to resize it (double-click the edge for its original size), and press − in its header to fold it away. The time controls and the career strip fold away with the arrow at their end.

Copy and paste

Select several things with Ctrl + click or a Ctrl + drag box, or press Ctrl + A for everything. Copy takes the glue, springs, wires and pipes between them along, and a device copied without the part it sits on stands on its own. Paste drops the copy under the pointer, and the copy stays selected so Move and Rotate can put it in place. Copies go through the system clipboard, so you can paste into another Red Sandbox tab. The Edit menu has the same commands.
